TilePivot — Privacy Policy

Last updated November 7, 2026

The short version: TilePivot collects no personal data. TilePivot does not have the Android internet permission. It requests no Android permissions at all, so it cannot send anything off your device. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no third-party SDKs. Everything TilePivot does happens on your device. One optional feature, described below, can use notification access if you turn it on yourself in Android's settings; it is off unless you do.

What TilePivot uses, and why

Your installed apps. TilePivot is a home-screen launcher. It reads the list of launchable apps on your device through Android's standard launcher APIs so it can show them as tiles. That list stays on your device.

Your own settings. Your pins, app folders, recently opened apps, theme, tile size and other preferences are stored only in TilePivot's private storage on your device. They are deleted when you uninstall TilePivot or clear its data. They leave TilePivot only if you use the in-app "back up settings" option, which saves them to a file in a location you choose. TilePivot does not upload that file anywhere.

A games folder, only if you pick one. If you choose a folder for the games library, TilePivot gets read-only access to that one folder, through Android's system folder picker. It reads file names and file headers there to list your games and show cover images. It never writes to the folder and cannot see anything outside it. You can remove the folder in TilePivot's settings, and you can revoke the access in Android's system settings at any time. The folder choice is not included in TilePivot's settings backup.

How often you open apps (for the home avatar). The avatar on your home screen can step in front of the app you have opened most this week. To know which app that is, TilePivot counts how many times you open each app from the launcher, per day. Only the app's package name and a count are kept, never what you do inside the app, and only for the last 14 days; older days are deleted automatically. The counts stay in TilePivot's private storage on your device. They are not included in the "back up settings" file.

Notification access, only if you turn it on. The home avatar can walk over to an app's tile when that app posts a notification. This needs notification access, which TilePivot never has unless you grant it yourself in Android's settings, and you can revoke it there at any time. When it is on, TilePivot uses only the name of the app that posted the notification, to find its tile. It never reads the notification's title, text, images or any other content, and it stores nothing about notifications.

Battery level and time of day. The home avatar looks sleepy late at night and when the battery is low, and perks up when you plug in the charger. TilePivot reads the battery level and charging state from Android's standard battery broadcast and the time from your device clock. Neither is stored.

Your avatar and profile. Your avatar's look, where you placed it on the screen, and the profile name and picture you choose are stored only in TilePivot's private storage on your device.

Launching a game. When you open a game, TilePivot hands its file path and title to the separate emulator app you installed (for example Xendroid+), on your device. That app is not part of TilePivot and has its own privacy practices.

Data sharing

TilePivot shares no data with anyone. It collects none, and it has no way to send any.

Children

TilePivot is a general-audience utility and is not directed at children.

Not affiliated

TilePivot is not affiliated with Microsoft or Xbox.
